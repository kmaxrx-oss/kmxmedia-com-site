## Funnel copy (Search-to-AI)
### Top ~30 percent — SERP intent match
The WordPress message “There has been a critical error on this website” means WordPress encountered a fatal problem and could not complete the page request. The message identifies a failure state, not the exact cause. A plugin, theme, custom code, PHP compatibility, memory condition, or update interaction may be involved.

Record where the message appears, when it began, and what changed immediately beforehand. The public site, administrator area, or only one page may be affected.

### Middle ~40 percent — why ordinary sites fail this job
Do not respond by deleting random files, repeatedly running updates, replacing the theme, or disabling many components without a backup and rollback path. Those actions can change the evidence or create additional failures. Do not paste passwords into a public form.

KMX needs the website address, exact visible message, affected areas, approximate start time, recent plugin or theme updates, hosting or PHP changes if known, backup status, administrator and hosting access availability, and the business impact. Diagnosis then follows the evidence from the recoverable state.

### Bottom ~30 percent — handoff into project brief /start/
[Start the relevant project brief](/start/?problem=wordpress-critical-error&source=wordpress-critical-error-repair). Begin with **Rescue — WordPress Critical Error** already selected. The emergency brief asks only critical-error questions before any redesign or marketing goals. Indicate whether the site is completely unavailable, whether the administrator area loads, what happened immediately before the failure, and whether bookings, payments, or inquiries are being lost.

The output is a structured triage summary and next diagnostic path. It is not a guaranteed instant fix or a cause inferred from the message alone.
